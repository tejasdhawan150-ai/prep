Canada PR Process from India: Express Entry Step by Step

Most skilled workers in India apply for Canada PR through Express Entry. You check which program you qualify for, take an approved language test such as IELTS General Training, get your education assessed, and create an Express Entry profile. Candidates are ranked by Comprehensive Ranking System (CRS) points and the highest-ranked are invited to apply. After an invitation, you have 60 days to submit your full application with documents, medical exam and fees.

The Canada PR process through Express Entry

  1. Check which program you qualify for. Express Entry manages three federal programs. Most applicants from India use the Federal Skilled Worker Program.
  2. Take an approved language test. In English: IELTS General Training, CELPIP-General or PTE Core. In French: TEF Canada or TCF Canada.
  3. Get your education assessed. An ECA shows what your Indian degree is worth in Canada.
  4. Prepare proof of funds if your program needs it.
  5. Create your Express Entry profile. You have 60 days to complete and submit it. If you are eligible, you enter the pool and get a CRS score.
  6. Wait for an invitation to apply (ITA). IRCC invites the highest-ranked candidates in regular rounds.
  7. Submit your PR application within 60 days of the invitation, with all documents, your medical exam and fees.
  8. Give biometrics and wait for a decision.

Step 1: Which Express Entry program fits you?

Express Entry programs and their minimum language level
ProgramMinimum language level
Federal Skilled Worker ProgramCLB 7 in all four skills (IELTS General Training 6.0 in each)
Canadian Experience ClassCLB 7 for TEER 0 or 1 jobs; CLB 5 for TEER 2 or 3 jobs
Federal Skilled Trades ProgramCLB 5 in Listening and Speaking; CLB 4 in Reading and Writing

Each program has its own work experience rules as well. Check the official program pages before you plan your tests, because the program sets your minimum language level.

Step 2: The language test, your biggest controllable factor

Your test result must be less than 2 years old when you create your profile and when you submit your application. IRCC converts each skill to a CLB level on its own, so one weak skill lowers your score even if the overall band looks good.

Language is also where most candidates can still gain points. Moving from CLB 7 to CLB 9 in all four skills adds 56 CRS points for a single applicant, and CLB 9 also raises your skill transferability points. French at NCLC 7 in all four skills adds a 50-point bonus when your English is CLB 5 or higher.

Step 3: Educational credential assessment

If you studied outside Canada, you need an ECA to qualify as a Federal Skilled Worker principal applicant, or to get CRS points for that education. The report must be less than 5 years old when you create your profile and when you apply, so check it will stay valid while you are in the pool.

Step 4: Proof of funds

Federal Skilled Worker and Federal Skilled Trades applicants usually need to show settlement funds for themselves and their family, using official letters from their banks. You do not need proof of funds if you can already work legally in Canada and have a valid job offer. The minimum amounts depend on family size and are updated by IRCC, so check the current table.

Step 5 and 6: Your profile and the invitation rounds

Once your profile is submitted and you are eligible, you are placed in the pool and ranked by CRS points. IRCC holds rounds of invitations. Some are general, some are for one program, and some are category-based, inviting candidates with strong French or with experience in occupations IRCC has prioritised. Each round has its own cut-off score.

You pay the processing fee, the right of permanent residence fee and the biometrics fee when you apply. The online system will not let you submit until all mandatory documents and fields are complete, so start collecting documents while you are in the pool, not after the invitation.

Step 8: Biometrics and the decision

After you apply, you give your biometrics. IRCC then checks that you meet the program and category requirements, that the information in your profile was accurate, and that you are admissible to Canada.

What changes on 3 November 2026

IRCC has announced changes to how CRS points work for Canadian work experience and job offers, effective 3 November 2026:

  • A new Labour Market Integration factor, worth up to 150 points (140 with a spouse or partner), covering either Canadian work experience or a qualifying job offer, not both.
  • Job offers in eligible high-wage occupations can earn 100, 70 or 50 points, depending on the wage. Other job offers earn no CRS points.
  • Canadian work experience keeps its points for length, with a bonus of 20 to 70 points for at least a year in an eligible high-wage occupation.
  • An LMIA is no longer needed for a job offer to qualify, and it does not add points.

IRCC says invitation rounds may pause for up to two weeks while scores are recalculated. Language and education points are not part of this change, so a stronger language score is still the most reliable way for most candidates applying from India to raise their CRS.

Frequently asked questions

What is the Canada PR process from India?
Most skilled workers apply through Express Entry: check your program, take an approved language test, get an ECA, create a profile, receive an invitation, then submit your PR application within 60 days.
Is IELTS mandatory for Canada PR?
You need results from an approved language test. In English that is IELTS General Training, CELPIP-General or PTE Core. IELTS Academic is not accepted for Express Entry.
Do I need a job offer for Canada PR?
No. Express Entry does not require a job offer. From 3 November 2026, a job offer adds CRS points only if it is in an eligible high-wage occupation.
How long do I have after an invitation to apply?
60 calendar days to submit your complete application.
What is an ECA and do I need one?
An educational credential assessment shows what your foreign education is worth in Canada. You need it to qualify as a Federal Skilled Worker principal applicant or to get CRS points for education from outside Canada.
How can I increase my CRS score?
Language is the factor most candidates can still change: moving from CLB 7 to CLB 9 adds 56 points for a single applicant, and French at NCLC 7 can add a 50-point bonus.
