IELTS Speaking Introduction: What the Examiner Asks First

IELTS Speaking starts with a short introduction: the examiner introduces themselves, asks your full name and what to call you, and checks your ID. Then Part 1 begins, usually with questions about your work or studies, where you live or your hometown. These first answers are not a separate score, but they set the tone: answer naturally in 2 or 3 sentences, with a reason or example, and do not recite a memorised self-introduction.

What the examiner says

The wording varies, but the opening usually goes like this:

  • "Good morning. My name is ... Can you tell me your full name, please?"
  • "And what can I call you?"
  • "Can I see your identification, please?" (show the passport you registered with)
  • "Now, in this first part, I'd like to ask you some questions about yourself. Let's talk about what you do. Do you work or are you a student?"

Sample answers

Can you tell me your full name? "My full name is Harpreet Kaur Sandhu." Just say your name; no need for more.

What can I call you? "You can call me Harpreet."

Do you work or are you a student? "I work as an accountant at a mid-sized logistics company in Ludhiana. I've been there for about three years, and I mostly handle tax filings and monthly reports."

Where are you from? "I'm from Patiala, a city in Punjab that's known for its old palaces and, of course, the Patiala peg. I've lived there most of my life, apart from four years in Chandigarh for college."

Do you live in a house or a flat? "We live in a two-storey house with my parents. It isn't very big, but it has a small terrace where I like to sit in the evenings."

How to start your Speaking test well

  • Do not give a long memorised introduction. The examiner asks specific questions; answer those.
  • Extend Part 1 answers to 2 or 3 sentences: answer, reason, example.
  • Use natural contractions such as I'm and I've.
  • It is fine to ask the examiner to repeat a question in Part 1.
  • Bring the same ID you registered with, usually your passport in India.

Frequently asked questions

What is the first question in IELTS Speaking?
After greeting you, the examiner asks your full name and what to call you, checks your ID, and then starts Part 1, usually with "Do you work or are you a student?" or a question about where you live.
Should I prepare a self-introduction for IELTS?
Not a long memorised one. The examiner asks short, specific questions. Answer each one naturally in 2 or 3 sentences.
Is the introduction part of the IELTS Speaking score?
There is no separate mark for it, but the examiner is listening from the start. Clear, natural answers help.
What ID do I need for IELTS Speaking?
The same ID you used to register, which in India is usually your passport.
How long is IELTS Speaking Part 1?
About 4 to 5 minutes, including the introduction.
