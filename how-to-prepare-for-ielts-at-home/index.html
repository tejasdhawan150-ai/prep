How to Prepare for IELTS at Home: A Self-Study Plan

You can prepare for IELTS at home if you follow a plan. Learn the test format, take one timed practice test to find your starting band, then spend most of your time on your weakest skill. Practise every section under real time limits, review every mistake, and take a full mock test every week. The hardest part to do alone is checking your own Writing and Speaking, so get those marked by someone who knows the band descriptors.

How to prepare for IELTS at home: 6 steps

  1. Know which test you are taking. General Training for Canada PR and most migration, Academic for university admission. Listening and Speaking are the same in both, but Reading and Writing Task 1 are different, so practise the right version.
  2. Learn the format. Listening has 40 questions in about 30 minutes. Reading has 40 questions in 60 minutes. Writing has two tasks in 60 minutes, and Speaking is an 11–14 minute interview in three parts.
  3. Find your starting band. Take one full practice test under real timing, with no pauses and no dictionary. Mark Listening and Reading strictly: a misspelt word or one word too many is wrong.
  4. Set a target and a date. Check the exact score your university, employer or visa needs, including any minimum in each skill. Book the real test only when your practice scores reach that target.
  5. Work on your weakest skill first. Your overall band is an average, so one weak skill pulls everything down. Give it about half your study time.
  6. Test yourself every week. One full timed mock test a week shows whether your plan is working. If a skill has not moved in two weeks, change how you practise it.

A 4-week home study plan

If you have a month, this plan covers the basics. If you have longer, repeat weeks 3 and 4 until your mock tests reach your target.

A 4-week IELTS study plan you can follow at home
WeekFocusWhat to do
Week 1Format and starting pointLearn the four sections and question types. Take one full timed practice test, mark it strictly and write down your band in each skill.
Week 2StructuresLearn the structure of Writing Task 1 (report or letter) and Task 2 (essay). Answer Speaking Part 1 and Part 2 questions aloud every day and record yourself.
Week 3Weakest skillSpend about half your study time on your weakest skill. Do timed single-section practice and review every mistake.
Week 4Full mocksTake two full timed mock tests. Review them, fix the repeated mistakes, and prepare your test-day routine.

A daily routine that works at home

Studying at home works best with a fixed time and a fixed routine. A short session every day beats one long session at the weekend, because Listening and Speaking improve with regular practice.

A 90-minute daily routine for studying at home
TimeActivity
20 minutesListening or Reading practice under time, then check every wrong answer
30 minutesWriting: one Task 1 or one Task 2, written within the real time limit
15 minutesSpeaking: answer three or four questions aloud, record them and listen back
15 minutesReview: your mistake notebook, new words in context, and yesterday’s corrections
10 minutesPlan tomorrow: one specific thing to fix

How to practise each skill at home

Listening

  • Use official practice recordings and answer under exam conditions: one listen only.
  • After checking your answers, listen again with the transcript and find exactly where you missed each answer.
  • Keep a list of words you misspell. Spelling mistakes cost marks.

Reading

  • Give yourself about 20 minutes per section, and stop when the time is up.
  • Practise the question types that cost you most, usually True/False/Not Given and matching headings.
  • For every wrong answer, find the sentence in the passage that proves the right answer.

Writing

  • Write complete answers within the real time limit: about 20 minutes for Task 1 and 40 minutes for Task 2.
  • Count your words. Task 1 needs at least 150 and Task 2 at least 250.
  • Compare your answer with the public IELTS band descriptors, criterion by criterion. Better still, get it marked by someone who knows them, because it is very hard to see your own mistakes.

Speaking

  • Answer Part 1, Part 2 and Part 3 questions aloud and record yourself on your phone.
  • Listen back for long pauses, repeated words and grammar mistakes, then answer the same question again.
  • Practise with a friend or family member asking the questions, so it feels like a real conversation.

Which materials should you use?

Use official materials first, because they match the real test most closely:

  • Official IELTS practice tests and sample questions, published free on the IELTS websites run by IDP and the British Council.
  • Cambridge’s official IELTS practice test books, which contain past-style full tests with answer keys and Listening recordings.
  • The public IELTS band descriptors, which explain exactly what examiners look for in Writing and Speaking.

Too many books and apps can slow you down. One reliable set of practice tests, used properly, is better than ten used quickly.

How to check your progress

  • Listening and Reading: count your correct answers out of 40 and convert them to a band. Track the number every week.
  • Writing: keep every essay. Every two weeks, compare a new essay with an old one against the band descriptors.
  • Speaking: compare a recording from week 1 with one from week 4. Listen for fewer pauses and more developed answers.

Common mistakes when preparing at home

  • Reading tips and watching videos instead of doing timed practice.
  • Practising without a timer, then running out of time in the real test.
  • Checking answers without understanding why they were wrong.
  • Avoiding the weakest skill because it feels uncomfortable.
  • Memorising model answers. Examiners are trained to spot them, and they rarely fit the question you get.
  • Writing essays that nobody marks, so the same mistakes repeat.

When self-study is not enough

Many people reach their target at home. If your Writing or Speaking band has stayed the same across two or three practice tests, the problem is usually something you cannot see in your own work: how you develop ideas, organise paragraphs or answer the question asked. Frequently asked questions

Can I prepare for IELTS at home without coaching?
Yes. Many people do, with a clear plan, official practice tests and regular timed mocks. The hardest part alone is getting feedback on Writing and Speaking.
How many hours a day should I study for IELTS at home?
One to two focused hours a day works well for most people. Regular daily practice is better than one long session a week.
How long does it take to prepare for IELTS at home?
It depends on the gap between your starting band and your target, and how many hours you study. Take a timed practice test first, then plan backwards from your test date.
What is the best material for IELTS preparation at home?
Official materials: the free practice tests on the IELTS websites, Cambridge’s official IELTS practice test books and the public band descriptors.
How can I check my IELTS Writing at home?
Compare it with the public IELTS band descriptors, criterion by criterion, and get it marked by someone who knows them. It is very hard to see your own mistakes.
When should I book the IELTS test?
When your timed practice tests are consistently at your target in every skill, with enough time left for a retake before your deadline.
