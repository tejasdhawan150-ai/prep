SOP for a Canada Student Visa: What to Write in Your Study Plan

For a Canada study permit, the "SOP" Indian students write is what IRCC calls a letter of explanation: a letter that helps the visa officer understand you and your goals. IRCC says it should explain why you want to study in Canada and show that you understand your responsibilities as an international student. It is different from the statement of purpose your college asks for, but both tell the same story: why this program, why Canada, how you will pay, and what you will do after.

Is an SOP the same as IRCC's letter of explanation?

No. Your college's statement of purpose is for admission; the letter you send IRCC is for the visa officer, and it has a different job. Both tell the same story, so they must not contradict each other.

What does the visa officer look for in your study plan?

That you are a genuine student, that you can pay for your studies and your stay, and that the plan you describe makes sense for you. IRCC says the most common reasons for refusal are doubts that an applicant can support themselves financially, and doubts that they will leave Canada at the end of their authorized stay. Your letter should answer:

  • Why this program, after your previous studies or work?
  • Why Canada and this institution, rather than a similar course in India? Some visa office checklists ask what research you did on options at home.
  • How you will pay: tuition, living costs and travel, who is paying, and where the money came from.
  • What you will do after, and how the program helps your career.
  • Your ties and responsibilities: family, property, a job or a business, and that you will follow the conditions of your permit.

Hoping to work or stay in Canada later? You do not have to hide it. Canadian law allows you to want permanent residence in future, as long as the officer is satisfied that you will leave at the end of your authorized stay if you are not allowed to remain.

What format should a Canada study plan follow?

A clear letter in your own words, in a logical order, that matches every document in your file. Many applicants use one to two pages. A simple order:

  1. About you: your education, work experience and any gaps, with what you did during them.
  2. The program: what you will study and why it fits your background.
  3. Why Canada and this institution: and why not a similar course at home.
  4. Funding: tuition paid or due, living costs, who supports you, and the source of the money.
  5. After the program: your career plan and your ties at home.
  6. Your responsibilities: staying enrolled, making progress and following work limits, such as up to 24 hours a week off campus during classes.

What mistakes get study plans refused?

Copied templates, plans that do not match your documents, and money or gaps that are not explained.

  • Copied or generic text. Officers read thousands of letters; a template with your name in it adds nothing.
  • Contradictions: a different program, cost or sponsor in the letter than in your acceptance letter or bank documents.
  • Unexplained deposits: large recent deposits need a clear source, such as a sale deed, an FD certificate or a loan sanction letter.
  • Unexplained gaps or a sudden change of field with no reason given.
  • Vague plans: "I want a better future" does not tell the officer anything.
  • Ignoring a previous refusal: if you were refused before, the new letter must answer each reason.

What if your study permit was refused before?

Read the reasons in your refusal letter and answer each one with new information or documents. IRCC's help centre says that applying again with the same information, even through a consultant, will likely not change a refusal. See what to do after a Canada visa refusal.

Frequently asked questions

Is an SOP mandatory for a Canada study permit?
IRCC does not list it as a mandatory form, but its study permit document guidance recommends a letter of explanation, so it is worth including one.
How long should an SOP for a Canada student visa be?
IRCC sets no fixed length. Some visa office checklists ask for a one-page personal statement, and many applicants use one to two pages. Clear and specific matters more than long.
What should I write about funds in my study plan?
Who is paying, how much is available for tuition and living costs, and where the money came from. Explain large recent deposits and attach the documents that prove them.
Can I mention that I want PR in Canada later?
You can. Canadian law allows you to hope for permanent residence later, as long as the officer is satisfied you will leave at the end of your authorized stay if you are not allowed to remain.
Should I explain a study gap in my SOP?
Yes. Say what you did during the gap, such as work or exam preparation, and attach proof like experience letters.
Can I use a template or a sample SOP?
Use samples only as a checklist. Officers read many copied letters, and a generic one does not help. Write in your own words, with your own facts.
Is the SOP for my college the same as the one for my visa?
No. The college SOP is for admission and focuses on academic fit. The letter for IRCC focuses on your plans, funds and responsibilities. They must not contradict each other.
My study permit was refused. Should I rewrite my SOP?
Yes, but rewriting alone is not enough. Answer each reason in your refusal letter with new information or documents; IRCC's help centre says reapplying with the same information will likely not change a refusal.
