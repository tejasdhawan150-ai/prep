How to Score 8 in IELTS Listening

To score 8 in IELTS Listening, you need about 35–36 correct answers out of 40; Band 9 needs 39–40. Most lost marks at this level come from spelling, plurals and word limits rather than understanding. Read ahead before each part, listen for corrections, and check every answer against the instructions.

The small mistakes that cost Band 8

  • Spelling: a misspelled answer is wrong, even if you understood it.
  • Plurals: "book" when the speaker said "books".
  • Word limit: "NO MORE THAN TWO WORDS" means three words is wrong.
  • Distractors: speakers often change their answer ("Tuesday… no, actually Wednesday").
  • Losing your place: missing one answer and then missing the next three while searching.

How to approach each part

  • Part 1 (everyday conversation): names, numbers, dates. Practise spelling and number dictation.
  • Part 2 (monologue): often maps or plans. Read the labels first and follow the direction words.
  • Part 3 (discussion): several speakers. Note who says what and watch for agreement and disagreement.
  • Part 4 (lecture): no break in the middle. Use the reading time to predict answer types.

How to practise Listening for Band 8

  1. Do full tests under real conditions, played once only.
  2. Check every wrong answer with the transcript to see why you missed it.
  3. Keep a list of words you misspell and practise them.
  4. Listen daily to lectures, podcasts and news in English at normal speed.

For format details, see the IELTS Listening guide.

Frequently asked questions

How many mistakes can I make for Band 8 in Listening?
About four or five out of 40.
Is Listening the same for Academic and General Training?
Yes. Both use the same Listening test and the same score conversion.
Do capital letters matter in Listening answers?
Generally no, but spelling must be correct. Writing answers in capitals can help avoid confusion.
Is the recording played twice?
No. You hear each recording once, so reading ahead is essential.
Which part is the hardest?
Many students find Part 4 hardest because it is a continuous academic lecture with no break.
