Canadian Experience Class (CEC): Who Qualifies and How to Apply in 2026

The Canadian Experience Class (CEC) is the Express Entry program for skilled workers who already have Canadian work experience. You need at least 1 year (1,560 hours) of paid skilled work in Canada in TEER 0, 1, 2 or 3 jobs, in the 3 years before you apply, gained while you were authorised to work. You need CLB 7 for TEER 0 or 1 jobs, or CLB 5 for TEER 2 or 3. There is no education requirement and no proof of funds, and you must plan to live outside Quebec.

What work experience counts?

  • Skilled: in a TEER 0, 1, 2 or 3 occupation under the National Occupational Classification (NOC).
  • In Canada and paid: wages or commission. Volunteer work and unpaid internships do not count.
  • Authorised: gained while you had temporary resident status with permission to work.
  • Recent: within the 3 years before you apply.
  • Enough hours: 1,560 hours in total, which is 12 months at 30 hours a week. Part-time work counts if it adds up to the same hours, and you can combine jobs. Hours above 30 a week are not counted.

What does not count: self-employment (except some physicians), and work done while you were a full-time student, including co-op work terms. If you studied in Canada, see study in Canada to PR.

How CRS points work for CEC candidates

Your Canadian work experience earns CRS points: 40 for 1 year, rising to 80 for 5 years or more for a single applicant. From 3 November 2026, if at least 12 months of that experience were in an eligible high-wage occupation, you get a bonus of 70, 40 or 20 points, and IRCC counts the higher of your work experience points and your job offer points, up to 150. See the November 2026 CRS changes.

Language is still the biggest lever you control. Moving from CLB 7 to CLB 9 in all four skills adds 56 points for a single applicant, and strong French adds more. Many people working in Canada take CELPIP; see CELPIP coaching. Check recent cut-offs on Express Entry draws 2026.

How to apply through CEC

  1. Check your hours and NOC for every job you will claim.
  2. Take an approved language test and get your result.
  3. Create your Express Entry profile. If you meet the CEC rules, you enter the pool.
  4. Wait for an invitation in a round that includes CEC candidates.
  5. Apply within 60 days of the invitation. See what to do after an ITA.

Keep your temporary status valid while you wait. Extending a work permit is a separate application from PR, so plan it early if your permit ends soon.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need proof of funds for the Canadian Experience Class?
No. Canadian Experience Class applicants do not need to show proof of funds.
Does work during my studies count for CEC?
No. Work done while you were a full-time student, including co-op work terms, does not count towards the Canadian Experience Class.
Does self-employment count for CEC?
No. Self-employed work does not count, except for some physicians.
Does part-time work count for CEC?
Yes, if it adds up to 1,560 hours in the 3 years before you apply. Hours above 30 a week are not counted, and you can combine more than one job.
What CLB do I need for the Canadian Experience Class?
CLB 7 in all four skills if your experience is in a TEER 0 or 1 job, or CLB 5 if it is in a TEER 2 or 3 job.
Do I need a job offer for CEC?
No. A job offer is not required for the Canadian Experience Class.
Can I live in Quebec under CEC?
No. You must plan to live outside Quebec. Quebec selects its own skilled workers through separate programs.
