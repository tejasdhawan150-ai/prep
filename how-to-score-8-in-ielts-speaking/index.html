How to Score 8 in IELTS Speaking

To score 8 in IELTS Speaking, speak fluently with only occasional repetition or self-correction, use a wide range of vocabulary and idiomatic language accurately, mix complex and simple sentences with most of them error-free, and stay easy to understand throughout. Natural, extended answers matter more than memorised phrases.

What examiners listen for at Band 8

  • Fluency and Coherence: you speak fluently with only occasional repetition or self-correction. Hesitation is usually about what to say, not how to say it. Ideas are developed coherently.
  • Lexical Resource: a wide vocabulary used readily and flexibly to convey precise meaning, with skilful use of less common and idiomatic words and only occasional inaccuracies.
  • Grammatical Range and Accuracy: a wide range of structures used flexibly; most sentences are error-free.
  • Pronunciation: a wide range of features used well, easy to understand throughout. An accent is fine.

How to answer each part at Band 8

Part 1

Answer, then add one reason or example. Two to three sentences is right. One-word answers and long speeches both hurt.

Part 2

Use the one minute to note keywords, not full sentences. Speak for the full two minutes, telling it as a story with details: when, where, what happened, how you felt.

Part 3

This is where Band 8 is decided. Give a view, explain it, compare it with another view, and give an example. Use language for speculating ("It may be that…") and comparing ("Compared with…").

What keeps strong speakers at 7

  • Memorised answers. Examiners notice, and fluency drops when the question changes slightly.
  • Short Part 3 answers that do not explain or compare.
  • Repeating the same linking words ("Actually", "Basically").
  • Small, repeated grammar slips such as tense errors when telling a story.

How to practise Speaking for Band 8

  1. Record full Part 2 and Part 3 answers and listen back for repeated errors.
  2. Get a mock interview with a trainer that recreates the real exam pressure.
  3. Practise talking about abstract topics (education, technology, environment) for Part 3.
  4. Build topic vocabulary as phrases, not single words.

Frequently asked questions

Does my accent affect my Speaking score?
No, as long as you are easy to understand. Pronunciation is about clarity and features such as stress and intonation, not about sounding British or American.
How long should my Part 1 answers be?
Usually two to three sentences: an answer plus a reason or example.
Can I ask the examiner to repeat a question?
Yes, in Parts 1 and 3 you can ask for a question to be repeated or explained. It does not lose marks if you do it naturally.
Should I use idioms for Band 8?
Only ones you use naturally and correctly. A wrong idiom can lower your score.
Is it bad to pause while speaking?
Pausing to think about ideas is normal at Band 8. Frequent pauses to find words or grammar lower your Fluency score.
