IELTS Listening: Format, Question Types and How to Improve Your Band

What is the IELTS Listening format?

You listen to four recordings and answer 40 questions. Each recording is played once. The questions follow the order of the recording, which helps you keep your place.

Before each part you get a short time to read the questions. Use it to predict the type of answer: a number, a name, a place, a noun or a verb.

What happens in each of the four parts?

The parts get harder as you go. Parts 1 and 2 are everyday situations; Parts 3 and 4 are educational or training contexts.

  1. Part 1 — a conversation between two people in an everyday setting, such as booking a room. Expect names, numbers, dates and addresses.
  2. Part 2 — one person speaking about an everyday topic, such as a tour or local facilities. Maps and plans often appear here.
  3. Part 3 — a conversation between up to four people in an educational setting, such as students discussing an assignment with a tutor. Speakers agree, disagree and change their minds.
  4. Part 4 — a talk on an academic subject, like a short lecture. There is no break in the middle, so you must follow a long piece of speech.

What question types appear in IELTS Listening?

You will see a mix of gap-fill, multiple-choice and matching questions. Each type needs a slightly different listening skill.

  • Form, note, table, flow-chart and summary completion — fill gaps with words from the recording. Word limits apply.
  • Sentence completion and short-answer questions — also word-limited.
  • Multiple choice — one answer, or more than one from a longer list. Speakers often mention wrong options first.
  • Matching — link items (people, opinions, features) to a list of options.
  • Plan, map and diagram labelling — follow directions and positions.

What are the most common IELTS Listening mistakes?

Most lost marks are not about understanding. They come from spelling, grammar and instructions. These are the easiest marks to win back.

  • Spelling. A misspelt answer is marked wrong. Practise common words (accommodation, environment, necessary), days, months and spelled-out names.
  • Plurals. If the speaker says "bicycles", "bicycle" is wrong. Check if the gap needs a singular or plural.
  • Word limits. "NO MORE THAN TWO WORDS AND/OR A NUMBER" means exactly that. Three words is wrong, even if the meaning is right.
  • Distractors. Speakers correct themselves: "Tuesday — no, sorry, Wednesday." Wait for the final answer.
  • Losing your place. If you miss one answer, move on. Chasing it costs the next three.
  • Grammar in the gap. The answer must fit the sentence. Read around the gap to check.

How do you practise IELTS Listening effectively?

Do full practice tests under exam conditions, then review every mistake by listening again with the transcript. The review is where you actually improve.

  1. Take one full test, timed, with one play only. Mark it honestly.
  2. Sort each mistake: spelling, plural, word limit, distractor, or did not hear it.
  3. Replay with the transcript. Find the exact words you missed and why.
  4. Fix the pattern. If spelling costs you three marks a test, drill spelling for a week.
  5. Build daily listening with podcasts, lectures and news in different accents. Try writing down numbers and names you hear.
  6. Repeat. Track your raw score out of 40 each week.

Frequently asked questions

How long is IELTS Listening?
About 30 minutes of recordings. On paper you get extra time at the end to transfer your answers; on computer you type as you go and have a short time to check.
Is the recording played twice?
No. Each recording is played once, so reading the questions in advance matters.
Is Listening the same for Academic and General Training?
Yes. Both versions take the same Listening test.
Does spelling count in IELTS Listening?
Yes. A misspelt answer is marked wrong, so practise spelling common words, names and numbers.
How many answers do I need for Band 7 in Listening?
About 30 out of 40. The exact conversion can vary slightly, so treat this as approximate.
Can I write in capital letters?
Yes. On the paper test you can write answers in capitals, which many people find clearer.
