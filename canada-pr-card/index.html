Canada PR Card: First Card, Renewal and Travel

A PR card proves you are a permanent resident of Canada when you travel. Your first card is free if you send your photo and Canadian address within 180 days of becoming a permanent resident, and it is mailed only to an address in Canada. Most cards are valid for 5 years. Renew when yours will expire in less than 9 months; a renewal or replacement costs CAD 50. An expired card does not end your PR status, but you cannot travel on it: if you are outside Canada without a valid card, you need a permanent resident travel document (PRTD) to fly back.

How you get your first PR card

Your first PR card is made after you become a permanent resident. IRCC needs your photo and a mailing address in Canada. If you send them within 180 days of becoming a permanent resident, the first card is free. IRCC can only mail a PR card to an address in Canada, so if you move, update your address: a card that is not collected within 180 days of a change of address is destroyed, and you would have to apply and pay again.

When and how to renew your PR card

Apply to renew when your card will expire in less than 9 months, or after it has expired. IRCC rejects renewal applications from people whose card is still valid for more than 9 months, unless their name has changed. When you renew, you report your trips outside Canada for up to the last 5 years and show that you meet the residency obligation. The fee is CAD 50 per person, paid online.

The 730-day residency obligation

To keep PR status, you must be physically present in Canada for at least 730 days in every 5-year period. The days do not have to be continuous. When you renew your card, you show proof that you met this rule, such as pay stubs, bank statements or a tax notice of assessment.

Outside Canada without a valid PR card? You need a PRTD

An expired PR card cannot be used as a travel document. If you are outside Canada and your card has expired or been lost, apply for a permanent resident travel document (PRTD) before you fly back. It costs CAD 50 and is normally valid for a single entry. When you apply for a PRTD, an officer also checks whether you have met the residency obligation, so plan your time abroad with the 730-day rule in mind.

An expired card does not mean you lost PR

IRCC says you do not lose permanent resident status when your PR card expires. Status ends only in specific cases, such as an officer deciding you are no longer a permanent resident after an inquiry or a refused PRTD appeal, or if you give up your status voluntarily. Still, keep your card valid if you travel, so you can board a flight to Canada without delay.

Frequently asked questions

Is the first Canada PR card free?
Yes, if you send your photo and an address in Canada within 180 days of becoming a permanent resident. Renewals and replacements cost CAD 50.
How long is a Canada PR card valid?
Most PR cards are valid for 5 years. Some are valid for 1 year.
When should I renew my PR card?
When it will expire in less than 9 months, or after it has expired. IRCC rejects renewals for cards valid for more than 9 months, unless your name has changed.
Do I lose PR status if my PR card expires?
No. IRCC says an expired card does not end your PR status. But you cannot travel on an expired card, so you may need a PR travel document to return to Canada.
Can my PR card be mailed to India?
No. IRCC mails PR cards only to addresses in Canada.
What is the 730-day rule for Canada PR?
Permanent residents must be physically present in Canada for at least 730 days in every 5-year period. The days do not need to be continuous.
