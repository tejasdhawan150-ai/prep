Best IELTS Books for Self-Study (and How to Use Them)

The most useful IELTS books are the ones written by the people who make the test: the Cambridge IELTS practice test series and The Official Cambridge Guide to IELTS. Add one vocabulary book and one grammar book, such as Cambridge Vocabulary for IELTS and Cambridge Grammar for IELTS, and use the free practice material from IELTS, the British Council and IDP. Books build skills, but they cannot mark your writing or speaking, so pair them with feedback from a trainer.

A simple study order

  1. Week 1: take one full Cambridge test under timed conditions to find your level.
  2. Weeks 2 to 5: work on your weakest skill with the Official Cambridge Guide, plus 20 minutes a day from the vocabulary or grammar book.
  3. Every week: one timed Reading and Listening test, and two Writing tasks.
  4. Last two weeks: full mock tests under exam conditions. See how to use an IELTS mock test.

What books cannot do

A book can check your Reading and Listening answers, but it cannot tell you why your essay is band 6 or how your pronunciation sounds. Most students who stay stuck at the same band are stuck in Writing or Speaking. That is where feedback from a trainer makes the difference. Frequently asked questions

Which book is best for IELTS preparation?
Start with the Cambridge IELTS practice test series and The Official Cambridge Guide to IELTS, both from the people who make the test.
Are Cambridge IELTS books enough to prepare?
They are excellent for practice tests, especially Reading and Listening. For Writing and Speaking, you also need feedback on your own answers.
Which Cambridge IELTS book should I start with?
Use the most recent volumes first, since they reflect the current test, then older ones for extra practice.
Are there free IELTS practice materials?
Yes. IELTS, the British Council and IDP all publish free practice tests and sample questions online.
Do I need separate books for General Training?
Cambridge IELTS volumes include General Training Reading and Writing tests. Listening and Speaking are the same for both versions.
