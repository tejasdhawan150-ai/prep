IELTS Cue Cards September to December 2026 (Practice Topics and Answers)

Here are IELTS Speaking Part 2 cue cards to practise for September to December 2026, with original band 8-style sample answers. IELTS does not publish its cue cards, and lists of "new cue cards" online are based on what candidates remember. So use these as practice in the same style: each card gives you 1 minute to prepare and up to 2 minutes to speak, followed by a few Part 3 questions on the same theme. Practise speaking for the full 2 minutes, out loud, with a timer.

How to answer a cue card in 2 minutes

  1. Use the minute well: write 4 to 6 keywords, one for each bullet, plus one detail or story.
  2. Start directly: "I'd like to talk about..." and answer the first bullet.
  3. Tell it like a story: when, where, who, what happened. Past tenses carry most cue cards.
  4. Finish with the "explain" bullet, which is where you show opinions and range.
  5. Keep going until the examiner stops you. Running out at 1 minute is the most common reason Part 2 scores drop.

6 practice cue cards with sample answers

1. Describe a time you helped someone

You should say:

  • who you helped
  • what the problem was
  • how you helped
  • and explain how you felt about it
Band 8-style sample answer

Last winter my neighbour, an elderly man who lives alone, had to renew his pension paperwork online, and he was quite stressed because the website kept logging him out. I offered to help one Sunday morning. We sat at his dining table with his documents, and I realised the problem was simply that his browser was out of date. Once I updated it, we filled in the form together, step by step, and I showed him how to save the confirmation as a PDF. The whole thing took about an hour. What I remember most is his relief: he insisted on making me tea and kept saying he would have paid an agent to do it. It made me feel useful, and it reminded me that small, practical help can matter more than big gestures.

2. Describe a place in your city you like to visit

You should say:

  • where it is
  • how often you go there
  • what you do there
  • and explain why you like it
Band 8-style sample answer

The place I like most in my city is a small public library near the old market. It is on the first floor of a municipal building, so most people walk past without noticing it. I go there almost every Saturday afternoon. I usually read newspapers for a while, then pick a novel or a travel book to borrow. The reading room has big windows and ceiling fans, and it is always quiet, which is rare in our busy city. I like it because it feels like a break from screens and noise. It also has a friendly librarian who remembers what I read and suggests new authors, which makes the place feel personal.

3. Describe a skill you learned recently

You should say:

  • what the skill is
  • how you learned it
  • how long it took
  • and explain how it has helped you
Band 8-style sample answer

A skill I picked up recently is cooking simple North Indian meals. I had always depended on my mother, but when I started working in another city I had to feed myself. I learned mostly from short videos and from calling my mother while I cooked, which she found very funny. It took about two months before I could make dal, rice and a vegetable dish without burning anything. It has helped me in several ways: I save money, I eat healthier, and I feel more independent. It has also given me a new hobby, because I now enjoy trying one new recipe every weekend.

4. Describe a piece of technology you find useful

You should say:

  • what it is
  • when you got it
  • how you use it
  • and explain why it is useful
Band 8-style sample answer

The piece of technology I find most useful is a pair of wireless earphones I bought about a year ago. I use them every day: on my commute, at the gym and in online meetings. Their main advantage is noise cancellation, which blocks out traffic and lets me listen to podcasts or practise English listening even on a crowded bus. They are also handy for calls because I can talk while walking. I would say they are useful because they turn dead time, such as travel, into time I can learn something, and they have made my long commute much less tiring.

5. Describe a journey you remember well

You should say:

  • where you went
  • who you went with
  • how you travelled
  • and explain why you remember it
Band 8-style sample answer

One journey I remember well was a train trip from Delhi to Shimla with three college friends. We took an early train to Kalka and then the narrow-gauge toy train up the mountains. The second part was slow, about five hours, but the views were amazing: pine forests, tiny stations and more than a hundred tunnels. We played cards, shared snacks and kept leaning out to take photos. I remember it mainly because it was our last trip before we all started jobs in different cities, so it felt like the end of one chapter of our lives.

6. Describe a person who has a healthy lifestyle

You should say:

  • who the person is
  • what they do to stay healthy
  • how you know them
  • and explain how they influence you
Band 8-style sample answer

A person I admire for her healthy lifestyle is my aunt, who is in her fifties. She wakes up at six, walks for forty minutes every morning and does yoga three times a week. She eats home-cooked food, avoids sugary drinks and always finishes dinner early. I have known her all my life, and I stayed with her family for a month last year. What struck me is that she is never strict or preachy about it; she just makes healthy choices look normal. Since that month, I have started walking in the evenings and cutting down on fried snacks, so she has definitely influenced me.

What examiners listen for

Part 2 is marked on the same four criteria as the rest of Speaking: fluency and coherence, lexical resource (vocabulary), grammatical range and accuracy, and pronunciation, each worth a quarter of the score. Frequently asked questions

Where can I find the new IELTS cue cards for September to December 2026?
IELTS does not publish its cue cards. Lists of "new cue cards" are based on what test takers remember. Practise cards in the same style, like the ones on this page, so you can handle any topic.
How long should I speak in IELTS Part 2?
Up to 2 minutes. The examiner will stop you. Aim to keep talking until then, because short answers lower your fluency score.
How long do I get to prepare a cue card?
1 minute, and you are given paper and a pencil to make notes.
Can I memorise cue card answers?
No. Examiners are trained to spot memorised answers, and they can lower your score. Practise ideas and vocabulary for common themes instead.
What comes after the cue card?
Part 3, a two-way discussion of more general questions on the same theme, lasting about 4 to 5 minutes.
Are cue cards the same for Academic and General Training?
Yes. The Speaking test is the same for both versions of IELTS.
