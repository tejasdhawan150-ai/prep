IELTS Reading: Academic vs General, Question Types and Time Strategy

IELTS Reading lasts 60 minutes and has 40 questions in 3 sections. There is no extra time to transfer answers. Academic Reading uses three long texts from books, journals and newspapers. General Training uses shorter everyday and workplace texts first, then one longer text. Aim for about 20 minutes per section.

What is the IELTS Reading format?

You get 60 minutes to answer 40 questions across three sections. Unlike Listening on paper, there is no extra time to copy answers onto the answer sheet, so write them there as you go.

The question types are similar in both versions. What changes is the kind of text you read.

How do Academic and General Training Reading differ?

Academic uses three long, academic-style texts. General Training uses everyday and workplace texts that get longer and harder, ending with one longer general-interest text.

What question types appear in IELTS Reading?

There are around a dozen question types. Knowing what each one tests saves you minutes on exam day.

  • True / False / Not Given (facts) and Yes / No / Not Given (writer's opinions).
  • Matching headings — choose the heading that fits each paragraph's main idea.
  • Matching information — find which paragraph contains a given detail.
  • Matching features and sentence endings.
  • Multiple choice.
  • Sentence, summary, note, table and flow-chart completion — word limits apply.
  • Diagram label completion and short-answer questions.

Most question types follow the order of the text. Matching headings and matching information do not, which is why they take longer.

How should you manage time in IELTS Reading?

Give each section about 20 minutes, and spend slightly less on the first because it is usually easiest. Never leave a blank — there is no penalty for a wrong answer.

  1. Skim the text first (2–3 minutes). Get the topic and what each paragraph is about. Do not read every word.
  2. Read the questions, underline keywords. Names, numbers and unusual words are easiest to scan for.
  3. Scan for the part that answers each question, then read that part closely.
  4. Move on after about 90 seconds on one question. Mark it and come back.
  5. Keep 2 minutes at the end to fill any blanks and check word limits on the answer sheet.

Tip: matching headings is often faster if you do it after other questions on the same text, because by then you know the paragraphs well.

What are the common IELTS Reading traps?

Most wrong answers come from matching words instead of meaning, and from mixing up False and Not Given.

  • Word matching. The text uses synonyms. If the question says "decline", the text may say "fall". An option that repeats the text's exact words is often a trap.
  • False vs Not Given. False means the text says the opposite. Not Given means the text does not say it. Do not use your own knowledge.
  • Word limits and spelling. Copy words exactly from the text, and keep within the limit.
  • Qualifiers. Words like "all", "some", "usually" and "never" change the meaning of a statement.
  • Running out of time on Section 1. Spending too long early leaves the hardest section rushed.

Frequently asked questions

How long is IELTS Reading?
60 minutes for 40 questions in 3 sections. There is no extra time to transfer answers.
Is General Training Reading easier than Academic?
The texts are more everyday, but you usually need more correct answers for the same band, so it is not simply easier.
What is the difference between False and Not Given?
False means the text says the opposite of the statement. Not Given means the text does not give that information.
How long should I spend on each section?
About 20 minutes each. Spend a little less on the first section and save time for the hardest one.
Is there negative marking in IELTS Reading?
No. A wrong answer scores zero, the same as a blank, so always write an answer.
How many answers do I need for Band 7 in Academic Reading?
About 30 out of 40. The exact conversion can vary slightly, so treat this as approximate.
