Canada PR Documents Checklist (Express Entry, from India)

For an Express Entry profile you need a valid passport, an approved language test result and, for education outside Canada, an ECA. After an invitation you have 60 days to upload the rest: work reference letters, proof of funds if your program needs it, police certificates, a medical exam, photos and documents for family members. The personal checklist IRCC creates in your account is the final list for your application.

You do not upload documents to create a profile. You enter details from them, such as your test results and ECA reference number, so keep them ready and accurate.

Documents after an invitation to apply

After an invitation, you have 60 days to submit your full application. IRCC builds a personal document checklist in your account based on your answers. Most applicants from India need:

What should a work reference letter include?

For each job you claim, IRCC asks for a letter on company letterhead, signed by your supervisor or an HR officer, with the company’s address, phone number and email. It should show:

  • your job title, duties and responsibilities;
  • your dates of employment;
  • your hours per week;
  • your annual salary and benefits.

IRCC checks that you did the duties in the lead statement of the occupation code (NOC) you chose, and most of its main duties, so make sure your letter and profile describe the same work.

Police certificates from India

IRCC lists these ways to get an Indian police certificate: the passport office, the Indian embassy or consulate if you live abroad, a BLS International centre, or your district police. A character certificate is not accepted. Certificates can take time, so IRCC suggests requesting them as soon as your profile is in the pool. If one is delayed past your deadline, you can upload proof that you applied with a letter of explanation, but IRCC accepts this only in rare cases.

Medical exam

Your medical exam must be done by a panel physician approved by IRCC; an exam by your own doctor is not accepted. Follow the instructions IRCC gives you, and keep the confirmation the clinic gives you. IRCC usually needs medical exams for your family members too, including those not coming to Canada with you.

Documents for your spouse and children

Your spouse or partner and dependent children need passports, photos and medical exams, and family members aged 18 or older need police certificates. If your spouse took a language test or got an ECA to add points, include those results too.

Common document mistakes

  • Reference letters without hours per week or salary.
  • Names spelled differently across documents, with no explanation.
  • A language test or ECA that expired before you applied.
  • A missing police certificate from a country you lived in.
  • Translations that are not certified.

This guide is general information, not immigration advice. IRCC decides every application, and its rules can change.

Frequently asked questions

What documents are required for Canada PR from India?
For your profile: passport, language test results and an ECA. After an invitation: work reference letters, proof of funds if needed, police certificates, a medical exam, photos and documents for your family, such as marriage and birth certificates.
Is a police certificate needed for Canada PR from India?
Yes. You need one from every country where you spent 6 or more months in a row since age 18, including India.
Where do I get a police certificate in India for Canada?
From the passport office, the Indian embassy or consulate if you live abroad, a BLS International centre or the district police. A character certificate is not accepted.
What should an experience letter for Canada PR include?
Company letterhead and contact details, the signature of your supervisor or HR, your job title, duties, dates of employment, hours per week and salary.
How long do I have to upload documents after an ITA?
60 days from your invitation to submit your complete application.
Do Indian documents need translation for Canada PR?
Only documents that are not in English or French. They need a certified translation.
