TEF Canada: Format, Scoring and How to Prepare

What is TEF Canada?

TEF Canada (Test d'évaluation de français pour le Canada) is a French test designed for Canadian immigration and citizenship. It is developed by Le français des affaires, the French-language assessment arm of the Paris Île-de-France Chamber of Commerce and Industry (CCI Paris Île-de-France).

IRCC accepts TEF Canada for Express Entry. The other accepted French test is TCF Canada. For the bigger picture, read our guide to French for Canada PR.

What is the TEF Canada format?

For immigration, you take all four modules. Each one is scored separately.

  • Compréhension orale (listening): you hear recordings, from short announcements to longer conversations and interviews, and answer questions.
  • Compréhension écrite (reading): you read texts such as notices, emails and articles, and answer questions on meaning and detail.
  • Expression écrite (writing): you write in response to set tasks, for example continuing a short news story and writing a reasoned opinion.
  • Expression orale (speaking): a face-to-face exchange with an examiner. Typically you ask questions to get information and then try to persuade the examiner.

Listening and reading test understanding under time. Writing and speaking are marked by trained examiners against set criteria. Timings and task counts are set by the organiser, so check the official TEF Canada page for the current format before you book.

How is TEF Canada scored, and how does it relate to NCLC?

Each module gets its own score. IRCC then converts each score to an NCLC level using its official equivalency chart.

So a TEF Canada result is really four NCLC levels, one per skill. For the additional French CRS points, IRCC looks for NCLC 7 or higher in all four. A high reading score cannot make up for a low speaking score.

How long are TEF Canada results valid?

For Express Entry, your TEF Canada result must be less than 2 years old when you create your profile and when you submit your application. If your result will expire before you apply, plan a retake early.

How do you prepare for TEF Canada?

Build general French to around B1 first, then train for the test's tasks. Most lost marks come from speaking and writing, so give them the most practice.

  1. Know your level. A trainer's check tells you how far you are from NCLC 7 in each skill.
  2. Listen daily. French radio, podcasts and news train your ear for real speed and accents.
  3. Write to the task. Practise the news-story and opinion styles, and get each piece marked.
  4. Rehearse the speaking tasks. Practise asking questions naturally and arguing a point with an examiner.
  5. Take timed mocks. Work under the real time limits so nothing surprises you on test day.

What mistakes cost marks in TEF Canada?

  • Reading the question too fast. Many wrong answers are traps built on a word you recognised.
  • Memorised speaking answers. Examiners notice. They want a natural exchange, not a speech.
  • Weak structure in writing. An opinion without clear arguments and examples scores low even with good grammar.
  • Uneven preparation. Since the CRS bonus needs NCLC 7 in every skill, one weak module can undo the rest.

Frequently asked questions

Who runs TEF Canada?
TEF Canada is developed by Le français des affaires, part of the Paris Île-de-France Chamber of Commerce and Industry (CCI Paris Île-de-France).
Is TEF Canada accepted for Express Entry?
Yes. IRCC accepts TEF Canada and TCF Canada as French tests for Express Entry.
What are the four TEF Canada modules?
Listening (compréhension orale), reading (compréhension écrite), writing (expression écrite) and speaking (expression orale). For immigration you take all four.
What TEF Canada score do I need for NCLC 7?
IRCC publishes an official chart that converts each TEF Canada module score to an NCLC level. Check the current chart on the IRCC website, because it can be updated.
How long is a TEF Canada result valid for Express Entry?
Your result must be less than 2 years old when you create your Express Entry profile and when you apply.
Is TEF Canada easier than TCF Canada?
Neither is officially easier. They have different organisers and formats. Choose based on test dates near you and which format you are comfortable with.
