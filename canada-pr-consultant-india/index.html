Who is allowed to charge you for Canada PR help?

IRCC says anyone who advises or represents you for payment on an immigration matter must be authorised, and that this covers advice given before you apply as well as after. Authorised representatives are:

  • Regulated Canadian Immigration Consultants (RCICs), licensed by the College of Immigration and Citizenship Consultants;
  • lawyers and paralegals who are members of a Canadian provincial or territorial law society;
  • notaries who are members of the Chambre des notaires du Québec.

A relative or friend can help you for free without a licence. But if you pay someone who is not authorised, IRCC will not deal with them, and an application that names a paid representative who is not in good standing is returned. Many "visa agents" in India are not licensed, so check before you pay.

"The College" is the College of Immigration and Citizenship Consultants. "Accepted by IRCC" means IRCC will deal with them as your paid representative.

For an Express Entry or provincial nominee application, an RCIC or a lawyer can act for you. If your case goes to the Federal Court, for example a judicial review after a refusal, you need a lawyer. See what to do after a Canada PR refusal.

How to check a Canada PR consultant in 4 steps

  1. Ask for the full name and licence number. RCIC licence numbers start with R.
  2. Search the College's public register. The person should be listed by that name and shown as licensed and in good standing.
  3. Check who will sign your forms. IRCC's Use of a Representative form (IMM 5476) asks for the representative's name exactly as it appears on the College's register. The licensed person should be the one named on it.
  4. Make sure you can see your own application. Your documents and IRCC messages belong to you. Ask how you will see what is submitted in your name.

Does your consultant need to be in India?

No. An RCIC holds a Canadian licence, and it is the same licence wherever the consultant or the client lives. Express Entry profiles and applications are submitted online, so you do not have to visit a consultant's office. You still give biometrics and take your medical exam in person, near where you live.

What matters is that the person is on the College's register and that you can reach them when you need to. PrepEve is based in India and Canada, and Sanjita works with applicants online and on WhatsApp.

Red flags IRCC warns about

  • A promise of a visa, a job or PR. IRCC says no one can guarantee a job or a visa; only immigration officers decide.
  • Pressure to pay quickly, requests for large cash payments, or payment through text messages or social media.
  • Advice to hide a refusal, change your work dates, or use a reference letter for work you did not do. False information can lead to a refusal and a ban of at least 5 years, and it is your application that suffers.
  • Someone who will not put their name and licence number on your forms.

If an agent has already taken your money

  1. Stop paying and keep everything. Save receipts, bank transfers, messages and emails.
  2. Check the register. Find out whether the person is licensed.
  3. Report it to the College. You can complain about a licensed consultant, or report an unlicensed one, through the College's online portal. The College says it does not handle complaints that are only about getting fees back.
  4. Report fraud. IRCC advises reporting it to your local police and the Canadian Anti-Fraud Centre, and it has its own page for reporting immigration fraud.
  5. Find out what was filed in your name. You can ask IRCC for your own file under the Privacy Act, at no cost. False information in an application counts against you, even if someone else wrote it.

Do you need a consultant at all?

Not always. IRCC says you are not required to hire anyone, and that using a representative does not get your application special attention, faster processing or a more favourable outcome. Every application is assessed the same way.

You may be fine on your own if your work history is simple, your reference letters are easy to get, you have no earlier refusals, and you are comfortable reading IRCC's guides and checking every detail yourself.

A licensed consultant helps most when:

  • your CRS score is below recent cut-offs and you need a plan to raise it;
  • your work history is complicated: several employers, gaps, or duties that do not clearly match one occupation code (NOC);
  • you have had a visa or PR application refused before;
  • your spouse or partner's scores and documents are part of the plan;
  • you are choosing between Express Entry and a provincial program;
  • you have been invited and the 60 days are running. See what to do after an invitation.

Which Canada PR routes does this cover?

  • Express Entry: the Federal Skilled Worker Program, the Canadian Experience Class and the Federal Skilled Trades Program.
  • Category-based rounds, including French-language rounds. See category-based draws and French draws.

What to have ready for your first conversation

  • Your age and marital status, and your spouse or partner's details if you have one.
  • Your education: each degree or diploma, and whether you already have an ECA.
  • Your jobs in the last 10 years: job title, main duties, dates and hours per week.
  • Language test results, if you have any, with the test dates.
  • Any study or work in Canada, and whether you have a brother or sister there who is a citizen or permanent resident.
  • Any visa or permit refusal, for Canada or any other country.

You do not need all of this to start. A rough picture is enough for a first assessment, and you can check your score yourself with the CRS calculator.

What Canada PR costs: government fees and consultant fees

There are two separate costs, and it helps to keep them apart.

  • Government and test costs. These are the same whether or not you use a consultant. IRCC charges CAD 1,590 for the principal applicant, the same for a spouse or partner, and CAD 270 for each dependent child, plus CAD 85 per person for biometrics. You also pay for your language test, ECA, medical exam and police certificates. See Canada PR fees.
  • The consultant's fee. This depends on your route and how much of the work you want done. At PrepEve it is shared on request, after your assessment.

Whoever you hire, ask for a breakdown that separates their fee from IRCC's fees, and keep a receipt for every payment. IRCC's fees are paid to IRCC online when you submit your application.

7 questions to ask any consultant before you pay

  1. What is your full name and licence number?
  2. Will you be the person named on my Use of a Representative form?
  3. Which program do I qualify for, and what is my CRS score today?
  4. If my score is too low, what is the plan to raise it?
  5. What are IRCC's fees, and what is your fee?
  6. How will I see what is submitted in my name?
  7. Who do I contact with questions, and how quickly do you reply?

A licensed consultant should be able to answer all seven clearly. Be careful if any answer is a promise of PR.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need a consultant to apply for Canada PR?
No. IRCC says you do not need to hire a representative, and many people apply on their own. A licensed consultant helps you plan your points, prepare documents and avoid mistakes, but IRCC treats every application the same way.
How do I check if a Canada PR consultant is genuine?
Ask for their full name and licence number, then search the College of Immigration and Citizenship Consultants public register. The person should be listed and in good standing, and their name should match the one on your Use of a Representative form (IMM 5476).
Can an unlicensed agent in India charge me for PR help?
No. IRCC says anyone who gives immigration advice or represents you for payment must be authorised: an RCIC, a lawyer or paralegal in a Canadian law society, or a Quebec notary. IRCC will not deal with unauthorised paid representatives.
What is the IMM 5476 form?
It is IRCC's Use of a Representative form. You and your representative both sign it, and you upload it with your application. It tells IRCC who may act for you, and it is needed even if the person helping you is unpaid.
Can a consultant guarantee Canada PR?
No. Only IRCC officers decide applications, and invitation cut-off scores change from round to round. IRCC warns that a promise of a visa or PR is a sign of fraud.
How much does a Canada PR consultant cost?
Fees vary with the route and how much help you need. At PrepEve, fees are shared on request after an assessment. IRCC's own government fees are separate and are paid to IRCC when you apply.
Is Sanjita licensed?
Yes. Sanjita is a Regulated Canadian Immigration Consultant licensed by the College of Immigration and Citizenship Consultants since June 2022, licence R710792. You can check her status on the College's public register.
What does RCIC stand for?
Regulated Canadian Immigration Consultant. RCICs are licensed and regulated by the College of Immigration and Citizenship Consultants, and IRCC accepts them as paid representatives.
Does my Canada PR consultant need to be in India?
No. The RCIC licence is Canadian and is the same wherever the consultant or the client lives. Express Entry profiles and applications are submitted online, so what matters is that the consultant is on the College's public register.
Will a consultant make my application faster?
No. IRCC says an application with a representative does not get special attention, faster processing or a more favourable outcome. A consultant helps you avoid mistakes and missing documents.
What is the difference between an RCIC and an immigration lawyer?
Both are authorised to charge for immigration advice and to represent you with IRCC. RCICs are regulated by the College of Immigration and Citizenship Consultants, and lawyers by a Canadian law society. Only a lawyer can represent you in the Federal Court.
Can I change or cancel my consultant?
Yes. IRCC says you can change or cancel a representative at any time. You tell IRCC by sending a new Use of a Representative form (IMM 5476).
What should I do if an immigration agent cheated me?
Keep your receipts and messages, check whether the person is licensed, and report them to the College of Immigration and Citizenship Consultants through its online portal. IRCC also advises reporting fraud to your local police and the Canadian Anti-Fraud Centre.
