IELTS Reading Multiple Choice Questions: Strategy and Practice

In IELTS Reading multiple choice questions you choose the correct answer from options A, B, C or D, or sometimes choose two or three answers from a longer list. Questions follow the order of the text. The best strategy is to read the question stem first, find the part of the text it refers to, then rule out the options that are wrong, too extreme or not mentioned. Most wrong options use words from the text but change the meaning.

Types of multiple choice questions

  • One answer from four options (A–D): the most common type. The question may be a stem to complete or a direct question.
  • Two or three answers from a list: for example "Choose TWO letters, A–E". Each correct letter usually counts as one mark.
  • Best title or main idea: sometimes the last question asks for the best title for the whole passage.

A 5-step strategy

  1. Read the stem, not the options. Underline key words, especially names, dates and unusual words that are easy to find.
  2. Locate the part of the text. Questions follow the text order, so the answer to question 3 comes after question 2.
  3. Read that part carefully, a few sentences before and after.
  4. Eliminate options: cross out any that the text contradicts, that are not mentioned, or that are too strong (always, never, only).
  5. Choose the option that matches the meaning, often written with different words (paraphrase).

Traps examiners set

  • Word match traps: an option repeats exact words from the text but says something different.
  • True but not the answer: an option is correct according to the text but does not answer the question.
  • Extreme words: the text says "many" and the option says "all".
  • Opinion vs fact: the option gives the writer's opinion when the question asks what researchers found, or the reverse.

Practice question

Text: "Although early solar panels were expensive and inefficient, falling production costs over the last two decades have made them one of the cheapest sources of new electricity in many countries. However, storing energy for use at night remains a challenge."

Question: According to the text, what is the main problem with solar power today?

A. It is too expensive to produce. B. Panels are inefficient. C. Energy is hard to store for use at night. D. Few countries use it.

Show the answer

C. The text says storage "remains a challenge". A and B describe early panels, not today (a time trap). D is not stated; the text says "many countries".

Frequently asked questions

How many options are there in IELTS multiple choice questions?
Usually four (A to D) for one answer. Some questions ask you to choose two or three letters from a longer list.
Do IELTS multiple choice questions follow the text order?
Yes. The answers appear in the same order as the questions.
Is there negative marking in IELTS Reading?
No. A wrong answer scores zero, so always choose an option rather than leaving it blank.
How do I avoid traps in multiple choice?
Do not pick an option just because it repeats words from the text. Check it matches the meaning, and watch for extreme words such as always, never and only.
How long should I spend on a multiple choice question?
Reading gives you 60 minutes for 40 questions, so about a minute and a half each on average. Move on if you are stuck and come back.
