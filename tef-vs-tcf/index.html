TEF Canada vs TCF Canada: Which French Test Should You Take?

TEF Canada and TCF Canada are both accepted by IRCC for Express Entry, and both convert to NCLC levels. Neither is officially easier. Choose the one with test dates that fit your deadline, a format you are comfortable with, and practice material that suits how you learn.

What do TEF Canada and TCF Canada have in common?

A lot. For immigration purposes they do the same job.

  • Both are accepted by IRCC for Express Entry and other economic programs.
  • Both test listening, reading, writing and speaking.
  • Both results are converted to NCLC levels, one per skill, using IRCC's official charts.
  • For Express Entry, both must be less than 2 years old.
  • NCLC 7 in all four skills on either test counts the same for the additional French CRS points.

How are TEF Canada and TCF Canada different?

The main documented differences are the organiser, the style of some tasks and how scores are reported.

TEF Canada compared with TCF Canada
TEF CanadaTCF Canada
OrganiserLe français des affaires (CCI Paris Île-de-France)France Éducation international
Listening and readingQuestions on a range of everyday and work textsMultiple choice that gets harder as you go
WritingTasks such as continuing a news story and arguing a pointTasks from a short practical message to a longer argued text
SpeakingExchange with an examiner: getting information, then persuadingInterview with an examiner: conversation, exchange, then opinion
ScoresOwn scale per module, converted to NCLCOwn scale per test, converted to NCLC

Because each test reports scores on its own scale, compare them through NCLC only. For each test in depth, read our TEF Canada format guide and TCF Canada format guide.

Choose TEF Canada if…

  • A TEF Canada centre near you has a date before your deadline.
  • You like the persuasion style of speaking task and are comfortable leading a conversation.
  • You write well in narrative and opinion styles, such as continuing a news story.
  • You have found TEF practice material you trust and enjoy using.

Choose TCF Canada if…

  • TCF Canada dates near you fit your timeline better.
  • You are comfortable with multiple-choice questions that get harder as you go.
  • You prefer writing tasks that build from short messages to a longer argued text.
  • Your practice so far has used TCF-style material and you already know its pace.

How do you decide between them in practice?

Start with dates, then format. The best test is the one you can sit in time and prepare for properly.

  1. Check availability. Look at test centres and dates near you for both tests.
  2. Work back from your deadline. Leave time for a retake in case one skill falls short of NCLC 7.
  3. Try sample tasks from each. Notice which speaking and writing tasks feel natural.
  4. Commit and train. Switching tests late wastes practice time.

Frequently asked questions

Is TEF Canada or TCF Canada easier?
Neither is officially easier. They measure the same skills and both convert to NCLC. Many learners find one format suits them better, so try sample tasks from each.
Does IRCC prefer TEF Canada or TCF Canada?
No. IRCC accepts both for Express Entry and treats the resulting NCLC levels the same way.
Can I compare a TEF score with a TCF score directly?
Not directly. Each test reports scores on its own scale. Convert both to NCLC using IRCC's official charts and compare the NCLC levels.
Can I take both tests?
Yes, you can sit either or both. For Express Entry you choose one valid result to use in your profile.
Which test should I take if my deadline is close?
Usually the one with the earliest available date that still gives you time to prepare. Leave room for a retake if one skill falls short.
