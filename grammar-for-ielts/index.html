Grammar for IELTS: What Examiners Look For and Common Errors

Grammar is marked in IELTS Writing and Speaking as grammatical range and accuracy, one of four criteria, so it is about a quarter of each score. Examiners look for two things: range (a mix of simple and complex sentences) and accuracy (how many sentences are error-free). For band 7, you need a variety of complex structures and frequent error-free sentences. Most Indian test takers lose marks on articles, tenses, subject-verb agreement and prepositions.

Grammar that shows range

  • Complex sentences with although, while, whereas, unless, because.
  • Relative clauses: "People who work from home save time on travel."
  • Conditionals: "If governments invested more in public transport, traffic would fall."
  • Passive voice for processes and formal writing: "The data was collected over five years."
  • Present perfect for changes over time: "Online shopping has grown rapidly."
  • Modal verbs for softening claims: "This may lead to...", "It could be argued that..."

Range only helps if it is accurate. A simple correct sentence is better than a complex broken one.

How to improve your IELTS grammar

  1. Find your top three errors. Get a few essays marked and list the errors that repeat.
  2. Learn the rule, then rewrite. Correct the same sentences yourself, not just read the correction.
  3. Proofread for your errors in the last 3 minutes of Writing: articles, verb endings and plurals.
  4. Practise two or three complex structures until they are automatic in Speaking.

Frequently asked questions

How important is grammar in IELTS?
Grammatical range and accuracy is one of four criteria in both Writing and Speaking, so it is about a quarter of each score.
What grammar do I need for band 7 in IELTS?
A variety of complex structures used with control, and frequent error-free sentences, though a few errors can remain.
Which grammar mistakes are most common in IELTS?
Articles, subject-verb agreement, tenses (especially in Writing Task 1), uncountable nouns, prepositions and run-on sentences joined with commas.
Should I use complex sentences in IELTS?
Yes, but only ones you can write correctly. Accuracy matters as much as range.
Is grammar marked in IELTS Reading and Listening?
Not directly, but spelling and word form must be correct in your answers to score the mark.
